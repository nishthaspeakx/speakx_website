SpeakX vs Stimuler: AI English speaking practice compared

Stimuler alternative? See how SpeakX and Stimuler differ on AI conversation practice, feedback, Indian-language explanations, roleplays and price.

Quick answer

SpeakX is an AI English speaking app for Indian learners, made by Ivypods Technology in Gurugram. You practice on guided voice calls with Sia, its AI speaking partner, with explanations in 7 Indian languages and roleplays for interviews, office and daily life.

Stimuler is a voice-first AI English coach from Stimuler Private Limited. You record 60-second speeches or chat with its AI coach, and it returns detailed feedback on pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, fillers, pace and tone, along with an IELTS band prediction.

Pick SpeakX if you are starting from a low level, want Hindi or regional-language explanations, and prefer structured practice that builds everyday confidence.

Pick Stimuler if you already speak intermediate English, are comfortable reading feedback in English, and want exam-style scoring or very granular speech analytics.

The two apps

What SpeakX and Stimuler are each built for

SpeakX

SpeakX is built for Indian learners who want to speak English, not just study it. The core activity is a voice call with Sia, an AI partner who talks with you, corrects you and explains the fix in your own language: Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada or Malayalam. Paths run from CEFR Pre-A1 to advanced, so a complete beginner and a working professional both start in the right place, at about 15 minutes a day. SpeakX is a different company from Speak (speak.com), a US-based app.

Stimuler

Stimuler calls itself an AI English speaking coach with instant feedback. Its signature task is a 60-second impromptu speech: within 20 to 30 seconds the app scores your pronunciation, fluency, grammar and vocabulary and flags fillers, pace, pauses and tone. You can also chat with its AI coach, Sarah, on 100+ topics, follow a personalized plan, and take IELTS speaking mock tests with band prediction. It won Google Play's Best of 2023 India award in the Best with AI category under its earlier name, Stimuler- IELTS Speaking Coach.

How they work

Two different approaches to getting you speaking

How SpeakX works

Where Stimuler asks you to perform and then grades you, SpeakX asks you to talk and helps you keep talking. Each session is a voice call with Sia, who asks questions, listens, corrects a wrong tense or a missing article, and explains why in the language you chose at setup. If you think in Hindi or Tamil, you hear the fix in Hindi or Tamil, which removes the translation step that slows many first-time speakers down.

Practice is organized as roleplays rather than open prompts: a job interview, a conversation with your manager, a parent-teacher meeting, a phone call to a service center. The scenarios are built around situations Indian learners actually face, so the vocabulary and tone you practice are the ones you will use. Premium opens 100+ real-life scenarios and unlimited AI talk time with a 24x7 AI teacher.

SpeakX places you on a CEFR path from Pre-A1 to advanced. This matters here: a learner who cannot yet form a full sentence has a clear starting point, which Stimuler's official pages do not describe. A report tracks fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation, a Confidence Meter shows how comfortably you are speaking, and city leaderboards add a light competitive element.

The time commitment is modest, about 15 minutes a day, and the app is free to start. The ₹2 3-day Premium trial and ₹299 monthly plan are covered in the pricing section below.

How Stimuler works

Stimuler is voice-first in a different sense. The central loop is a speaking test: you get a prompt, speak for 60 seconds, and the app analyzes the recording. Its listing says results arrive in under 20 seconds and cover pronunciation, fluency, grammar and vocabulary. The site demo shows a mispronounced word with a retry button and a band score for grammar. A 360-degree view adds fillers, rhythm, tone and pauses, more detail than most speaking apps surface.

Beyond the test, Stimuler offers conversation with an AI coach named Sarah across 100+ topics, from family and hobbies to technology, and scenario practice its blog describes as IELTS, interview and workplace scenarios. Official pages do not list the scenarios in detail, so we cannot say how deep the roleplay catalog goes. Stimuler also builds a personalized plan from your goals and level, with video lessons and exercises for each skill.

The exam angle is strong. Stimuler's Play listing mentions over 60 complete IELTS practice tests, live video mock tests with an AI interviewer, and IELTS band prediction you can track over time. Its earlier store names, Stimuler- IELTS Speaking Coach and Stimuler- IELTS Speaking Buddy, tell you where it started.

Everything, including the feedback, is in English; the App Store lists English as the only language. Stimuler has Indian founders and INR pricing and serves learners in 200+ countries, but official pages do not describe Hindi or regional-language explanations. We record this as not verified rather than a confirmed gap.

Key differences

The 6 differences that matter most

  1. 1

    Starting level

    SpeakX begins at CEFR Pre-A1, so a learner who cannot yet hold a basic exchange has a path. Stimuler's listings target fluency improvement and IELTS, and we found no beginner-from-scratch path described officially. If you already speak in paragraphs, both apps are open to you; if not, SpeakX is the safer start.

  2. 2

    Language of explanation

    SpeakX explains corrections in Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada or Malayalam. Stimuler's feedback and interface are in English, and Indian-language guidance is not described on its site, Play or App Store pages. For a Hindi-medium or regional-language learner, this is often the deciding factor.

  3. 3

    Feedback style: coaching versus analytics

    SpeakX corrects you inside the conversation and keeps the call going, then summarizes in a progress report. Stimuler stops the clock, scores the recording, and shows fillers, pace, tone and pauses. One is closer to a patient teacher, the other to a detailed scorecard. They suit different temperaments.

  4. 4

    Exam focus

    Stimuler predicts IELTS bands, runs video mock tests and bundles 60+ practice tests. SpeakX does not position itself as an exam tool; it builds daily speaking confidence through roleplays. If a band score is your goal this year, this difference outweighs every other row in the table.

  5. 5

    Practice format

    SpeakX is built around two-way voice calls and roleplays for specific situations. Stimuler is built around 60-second speeches and topic chat, with scenario practice described more briefly. If you want to rehearse an interview or an office conversation line by line, SpeakX is more structured for that.

  6. 6

    Price transparency

    SpeakX publishes one plan: ₹2 for 3 days, then ₹299 a month. Stimuler has no public price page (its /pricing URL returned a 404 on 12 October 2026), and the App Store lists several overlapping SKUs from ₹149 a week to ₹1,999 a year. Both offer a free way to begin.

Honest fit

Where each app is the better choice

Where SpeakX may be a better fit

  • ✓Absolute beginners and Pre-A1 to A2 learners who need a structured path before they can benefit from detailed speech analytics.
  • ✓Hindi-medium and regional-language learners who want corrections explained in Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada or Malayalam.
  • ✓Job-seekers and office-goers who want to rehearse specific situations: interviews, manager conversations, phone calls, parent-teacher meetings.
  • ✓Learners who freeze when graded and do better with a partner who keeps the conversation moving and corrects gently.
  • ✓Anyone who wants a single, clearly published price (₹2 trial, then ₹299 a month) rather than a menu of in-app SKUs.

Where Stimuler may be a better fit

  • ✓IELTS speaking candidates who want band prediction, live video mock tests with an AI interviewer and 60+ practice tests.
  • ✓Intermediate speakers who want very detailed per-speech analytics: fillers, pace, tone, pauses and rhythm, not just the four core skills.
  • ✓Learners who like fast, measurable scoring after every attempt; Stimuler says results arrive in under 20 seconds.
  • ✓Learners outside India or who prefer an app with a global user base; Stimuler reports users in 200+ countries.
  • ✓Anyone who values third-party recognition: Google Play named Stimuler Best with AI in its Best of 2023 India list.

Experience

User experience and how each app gives feedback

Feedback is where the two apps feel most different in daily use. On SpeakX, you are on a call. When you slip, Sia corrects you in the flow of the conversation, in the language you chose, and nudges you to say the sentence again. After the session, a progress report shows how your fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation are moving, and a Confidence Meter gives a simple read on how freely you spoke. City leaderboards show how you compare with learners near you.

On Stimuler, you perform first and read the verdict after. The 60-second speech ends, and within 20 to 30 seconds the app returns a score for each skill plus a breakdown of fillers, pace, tone and pauses. The site demo shows a mispronounced word with a retry prompt and a band-style grammar score. Over time, the IELTS band estimate becomes your progress line. Analytical learners find this motivating; nervous speakers can find a scorecard after every attempt tiring.

Then there is the question of who can read the feedback. Stimuler's is in English. If you can already read a note about sentence rhythm and act on it, fine. If you are still building basic sentences, feedback you cannot fully read is feedback you cannot fully use. SpeakX's explanations in 7 Indian languages are a direct answer to that, and the reason the two apps serve different stages of the same journey.

On outcomes, SpeakX says that in just 2 months, 4 out of 5 learners start speaking English confidently. That is the company's own figure, not an independent study. Stimuler's site quotes a 4.8 rating and 13M+ downloads; its Play listing shows 4.6 from 217.6K reviews and a 1 crore+ download tier, and the Play figures are the ones we could verify directly.

FAQ

SpeakX vs Stimuler: common questions

What is a good Stimuler alternative for Indian learners?+

SpeakX is a reasonable alternative if you want conversation practice rather than speech scoring, and if you want corrections explained in Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada or Malayalam. It starts at CEFR Pre-A1 and uses roleplays for interviews, office and daily life. If your main goal is an IELTS band, Stimuler remains the more exam-focused of the two.

SpeakX or Stimuler: which gives better speaking feedback?+

They give different kinds. Stimuler returns granular analytics after each 60-second speech: pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, fillers, pace, tone and pauses, usually within 20 to 30 seconds. SpeakX corrects you during the voice call with Sia, explains the fix in your language, then tracks fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation in a progress report. Choose analytics if you are intermediate and self-driven; choose guided correction if you are still building confidence.

Does Stimuler or SpeakX explain in Indian languages?+

SpeakX explains in 7 Indian languages: Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam. Stimuler's site, Google Play and App Store pages describe feedback and interface in English, and the App Store lists English as its sole language. We found no Indian-language guidance described for Stimuler, so we mark it not verified rather than absent.

Which app is better for job interview English practice?+

SpeakX has dedicated interview roleplays where Sia plays the interviewer and corrects you as you answer. Stimuler lists interview scenarios, and its speech analysis helps cut fillers and fix pace, but its scenario catalog is only lightly described on official pages. For rehearsing the conversation itself, SpeakX is more structured; for polishing delivery, Stimuler's analytics help.

Is SpeakX useful for IELTS speaking practice?+

SpeakX is not an IELTS preparation tool; it does not predict band scores or run mock tests. What it builds is daily speaking fluency and confidence, the underlying skill the IELTS speaking test measures. Stimuler has exam-specific features: IELTS band prediction, live video mock tests with an AI interviewer and 60+ practice tests. For the exam itself, Stimuler is the more direct choice; some candidates use SpeakX first to get comfortable speaking at all.

Is Stimuler free to use?+

Stimuler's listings say it has free features for everyone, with a paid Pro tier. There is no public price page; the India App Store shows monthly plans at ₹199, ₹299 and ₹399 and annual plans at ₹699, ₹999 and ₹1,999 as of 12 October 2026. SpeakX is free to start, with Premium at ₹2 for 3 days and then ₹299 a month on the SpeakX website.

Can a complete beginner use Stimuler or SpeakX?+

SpeakX is designed for this: CEFR paths begin at Pre-A1, calls are guided, and explanations come in your own language. Stimuler's official pages target intermediate learners improving fluency or preparing for IELTS, and we found no beginner-from-scratch path described. A complete beginner will usually find SpeakX the easier start.

Is Stimuler an Indian app?+

Stimuler is made by Stimuler Private Limited, founded in 2022 by Indian founders; editorial coverage places it in Bengaluru, though the official site does not state a headquarters. It prices in rupees and won Google Play's Best of 2023 India award in the Best with AI category. SpeakX is made by Ivypods Technology in Gurugram.

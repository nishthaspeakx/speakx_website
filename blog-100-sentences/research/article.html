100 simple English sentences for daily use, with Hindi meaning

Deepank AgarwalSeptember 8, 202615 min read

These 100 simple English sentences cover the situations you meet every day - greetings, home, work, shopping, travel and goodbyes. Each one has its Hindi meaning and the moment you would actually say it. Learn five a day, say them aloud, and use them the same day. That is the whole method.

If you are learning English, one of the easiest ways to improve is to start with simple English sentences you can actually use every day. Below are 100 common English sentences with Hindi meanings for conversations at home, work, shopping, travel and other everyday situations.

Why should you learn simple English sentences for daily use?

Knowing individual English words is useful, but words alone do not help much when you need to speak.

For example, knowing help, you and please is different from being able to quickly say: Can you help me, please?

Learning complete sentences helps you understand how words work together, so you can respond without building the sentence from scratch each time. If you want the grammar behind that, our guide to English grammar sentence structure explains how a simple sentence is put together — but you do not need it to start.

For Hindi-first learners, learning English alongside familiar Hindi meanings makes the transition easier. The goal is not to translate every sentence forever. The goal is to understand the pattern and gradually use the English sentence naturally.

Seven mistakes Indian speakers make with these sentences

These are not grammar-book errors. They are the ones that sound off to a client or an interviewer, and almost everyone makes them.

Commonly saidSay this insteadWhy
I am having a fever.I have a fever.Verbs about states do not take the -ing form.
I have a doubt.I have a question.A doubt means you do not believe something.
Please revert back.Please reply.Revert means to go back to an earlier state.
What is your good name?What is your name?A direct translation of शुभ नाम. It confuses listeners outside India.
I am agree.I agree.Agree is already the verb, so it does not need am.
Discuss about it.Discuss it.Discuss does not take about.
Today morning I called.This morning I called.Use this morning, this evening, last night.

The first one trips up more learners than any other. If tenses are where you get stuck, our guide to English grammar tenses covers it properly.

How can you learn these simple English sentences faster?

Reading these 100 sentences once is not enough. The improvement comes when you use them while speaking. Try this daily routine.

Step 1: Learn five sentences

Pick only five each day. For example: I am on my way. I will call you later. Can you help me? I don’t understand. Please speak slowly.

Step 2: Say each sentence aloud

Read each one several times, out loud. Not silently. The goal is to make your mouth comfortable with the sentence. If particular sounds keep tripping you up, our guide on English pronunciation difficulty covers the ones Hindi speakers find hardest.

Step 3: Change the sentence

Take one sentence and make variations. From “I am working on it” you get: I am working on the report. I am working on the project. I am working on my presentation. This teaches you patterns, not just sentences.

Step 4: Use them in real conversations

Use at least one new sentence during your day — with a colleague, ordering food, with a shopkeeper, asking directions, with friends, at home. The more often you use a sentence, the easier it becomes to recall.

  • talking to a colleague
  • ordering food
  • speaking with a shopkeeper
  • asking for directions
  • talking to friends and family

Should you translate Hindi into English while speaking?

Translation helps beginners understand meaning, but you should gradually move away from translating every sentence in your head.

Instead of thinking “मुझे थोड़ा पानी चाहिए” → “I need some water” again and again, learn I need… as a ready-to-use pattern.

  • I need some water.
  • I need some help.
  • I need some time.
  • I need some rest.

This is one reason practising complete sentences is more useful than memorising isolated vocabulary.

How can you practise simple English sentences every day?

Set aside a short block each day. Fifteen minutes, split three ways:

  • 5 minutes - Read. Read five to ten sentences aloud.
  • 5 minutes - Speak. Make your own sentences using the same patterns.
  • 5 minutes - Converse. Use them in a short conversation with a partner or a speaking-practice tool.

The aim is simple: understand → speak → repeat → use. For the longer arc beyond these 100 sentences, see our 4-step plan for English speaking fluency.

What are the most important simple English sentences to learn first?

If you are a complete beginner, start with these ten:

  • How are you? - आप कैसे हैं?
  • I am fine. - मैं ठीक हूँ।
  • What are you doing? - आप क्या कर रहे हैं?
  • Where are you going? - आप कहाँ जा रहे हैं?
  • Can you help me? - क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं?
  • I don’t understand. - मुझे समझ नहीं आया।
  • Please speak slowly. - कृपया धीरे बोलिए।
  • Can you repeat that? - क्या आप उसे दोबारा कह सकते हैं?
  • I will call you later. - मैं आपको बाद में कॉल करूँगा/करूँगी।
  • Thank you for your help. - आपकी मदद के लिए धन्यवाद।

Once these feel natural, move to the next ten.

Frequently asked questions

What are simple English sentences?

Simple English sentences are short, easy-to-understand sentences used to communicate common ideas. They suit beginners because they can be practised and used in everyday conversations straight away.

How can I learn English sentences with Hindi meaning?

Start with a few common English sentences and their Hindi meanings. Say them aloud and create your own variations. Gradually work towards understanding the English directly instead of translating every sentence in your head.

How many English sentences should I learn in a day?

There is no fixed number. For beginners, five to ten a day is manageable if you actually practise them. Using the sentences matters more than memorising a long list.

Can these sentences help me speak English fluently?

They give you a foundation for everyday conversation, but memorising sentences alone does not create fluency. You also need regular speaking, listening, vocabulary and pronunciation practice. Our guide on how to speak English fluently covers the full plan.

How can I practise these English sentences at home?

Read them aloud, record yourself, create variations, and use them in imaginary conversations. Practising with a speaking partner or a speaking-practice app gives you feedback you cannot give yourself.

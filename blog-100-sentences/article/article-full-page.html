Why should you learn daily use English sentences instead of single words?

Knowing the words help, you and please is not the same as being able to say “Can you help me, please?” quickly when a stranger is waiting. Daily use sentences give you ready-made patterns, so you respond without building each sentence from scratch. If you want the grammar behind the patterns, our guide to English grammar sentence structure explains how a simple sentence is put together, but you do not need it to start.

For Hindi-first learners, seeing the Hindi meaning next to the English sentence makes the first step easier. The goal is not to translate forever. The goal is to learn the pattern, say it a few times, and then use the English sentence on its own.

Which English sentences are most useful for beginners?

If you are a complete beginner, start with these ten daily use English sentences. They cover greeting someone, asking for help, slowing a conversation down and ending it politely, which is most of what a first conversation needs.

  1. How are you? — आप कैसे हैं?
  2. I am fine, thank you. — मैं ठीक हूँ, धन्यवाद।
  3. What are you doing? — आप क्या कर रहे हैं?
  4. Where are you going? — आप कहाँ जा रहे हैं?
  5. Can you help me? — क्या आप मेरी मदद कर सकते हैं?
  6. I don’t understand. — मुझे समझ नहीं आया।
  7. Please speak slowly. — कृपया धीरे बोलिए।
  8. Can you repeat that, please? — क्या आप उसे दोबारा कह सकते हैं?
  9. I will call you later. — मैं आपको बाद में कॉल करूँगा। / करूँगी।
  10. Thank you for your help. — आपकी मदद के लिए धन्यवाद।

What mistakes do Hindi speakers make with daily English sentences?

These are not grammar-book errors. They are direct translations from Hindi that sound off to an interviewer or a client, and almost everyone makes them at first.

Seven common mistakes and the natural sentence
Commonly saidSay this insteadWhy
I am having a fever.I have a fever.Verbs about states do not take the -ing form.
I have a doubt.I have a question.A doubt means you do not believe something.
Please revert back.Please reply.Revert means to go back to an earlier state.
What is your good name?What is your name?A direct translation of शुभ नाम; it confuses listeners outside India.
I am agree.I agree.Agree is already the verb, so it does not need am.
Discuss about it.Discuss it.Discuss does not take about.
Today morning I called.This morning I called.Use this morning, this evening, last night.

How can I learn five English sentences every day?

Pick five sentences from one section, read each aloud three times, change one word in each to make your own version, and use at least one in a real conversation before the day ends. Five sentences a day means the whole list in twenty days, and sentences you have actually said are the ones you remember.

Step 1: Learn five sentences

Pick only five each day, from one situation. For example: I am on my way. I will call you later. Can you help me? I don’t understand. Please speak slowly.

Step 2: Say each sentence aloud

Read each one several times, out loud, not silently. The goal is to make your mouth comfortable with the sentence. If particular sounds keep tripping you up, our guide on English pronunciation difficulty covers the ones Hindi speakers find hardest.

Step 3: Change the sentence

Take one sentence and make variations. From “I am working on it” you get: I am working on the report. I am working on the project. I am working on my presentation. This teaches you patterns, not just sentences.

Step 4: Use them in real conversations

Use at least one new sentence during your day: with a colleague, while ordering food, with a shopkeeper, asking for directions, or at home. The more often you use a sentence, the easier it is to recall.

How can I stop translating every sentence from Hindi in my head?

Translation helps you understand a sentence the first time, but speaking becomes slow if you translate every sentence while talking. The fix is to learn patterns, not single sentences. Instead of thinking “मुझे थोड़ा पानी चाहिए” and then “I need some water” each time, learn I need… as a ready-to-use pattern:

  • I need some water.
  • I need some help.
  • I need some time.
  • I need some rest.

After a week of saying the pattern aloud, the English comes first. This is why practising complete sentences works better than memorising isolated vocabulary.

How can I practise daily use English sentences at home?

Practise at home by reading the sentences aloud, recording yourself on your phone, making your own variations, and using them in imaginary conversations: order food from an imaginary waiter, give directions to an imaginary auto driver. A speaking partner or a speaking-practice app adds the one thing you cannot give yourself: feedback. Our guide to practising spoken English alone at home has a full routine.

A simple 15-minute daily practice routine

  • 5 minutes, read: read five to ten sentences from one section aloud.
  • 5 minutes, speak: make your own sentences using the same patterns.
  • 5 minutes, converse: use them in a short conversation with a partner or a speaking-practice tool.

The aim is simple: understand, speak, repeat, use. For the longer arc beyond these 100 sentences, see our 4-step plan for English speaking fluency and the guide on how to speak English fluently.

Are the Hindi meanings literal translations?

No. Each Hindi meaning is the natural way a Hindi speaker would say the same thing, not a word-for-word translation. Where the Hindi verb changes with the speaker, both forms are given, for example रहा हूँ / रही हूँ.

Should I use the formal or the casual form?

The English sentences work in both formal and casual situations; English does not change “you” the way Hindi changes between आप and तुम. In the Hindi column we use the polite आप form, which is safe with anyone. With close friends or younger family you can switch the Hindi to तुम, but keep the same English sentence.

## Task A: SERP shapes

### english spelling rules (1,600 / KD 23)
Almost entirely numbered rules guides. Top domains: grammarist.com (12 rules + printable worksheet), grammar-monster.com (Top 10 + on-page multi-choice test), languagetool.org, orchidsinternationalschool.com (Indian kids site), englishclub.com, leverageedu.com (Indian, PDF, FAQ), perfectyourenglish.com, quillbot.com, italki.com, logicofenglish.com (phonics). No tools, no videos, no misspelled-word lists in the top set. Audience: students/ESL/general writers.
Question signals: "What are the 5 spelling rules in English?", "What is a famous spelling rule?", "How do I improve my spelling?", "Why is it important to know spelling rules?"

### practice spelling (6,600 / KD 50) — the zero-click query
SERP is tools, quizzes, printables and games: spellingtraining.com (type your own list, hear and spell), SpellQuiz, Spelling Stars (hear-type-check), Spell4Fun app, Microsoft Store app, Twinkl Look-Cover-Write-Check booklets, Education.com worksheets, Spellzone printables, howtospell.co.uk exercises, TalkDrill spelling bee, spelling-test.com. The only articles are Reading Rockets (a parents/teachers guide) and a McGraw-Hill study-skills chapter. Audience: overwhelmingly K-8 parents and teachers; a minority of adults/ESL.
What a searcher expects to click: something they can do in the next minute — a word list to self-test, a hear-and-type quiz, a printable, a game. SpeakX's result ("English Spelling Practice — Everything You Need to Know", H2s: Key Benefits, Career Growth and Salary Impact) reads as an article about practice, not a place to practise.

### practicing spelling — the other zero-click query
How-to/methods SERP, parent-and-teacher framed: Reading Rockets, a school Google Site "Practicing Spelling at Home", BookShark "4 Simple Ways", Oxford Owl "Twelve tips for weekly spellings", British Council Voices (young learners), TCK Publishing and YourDictionary spelling-test study tips, plus a few adult-facing posts (engVid video, Read and Spell, WritingBeginner 100+ tips & words). Nearly every result is about children's weekly spelling lists. Adult searchers are underserved — an adult routine plus a ready word list would stand out.

### difficult spellings in english (880 / KD 26)

### english spelling practice (590 / KD 38)
Mostly exercises: learnenglishfeelgood.com listen-and-type dictations (3 URLs), British Council Teens "How do you spell it?" quiz, Varsity Tutors quiz, two iOS apps, Spellzone, SpellQuiz, TalkDrill, Twinkl/Education.com worksheets, ezschool (kids). Two articles: FluentU "how to improve english spelling" and British Council India "How to improve your English spelling" — the only India-targeted page in any of these SERPs.

### how to write correct spelling in english (320 / KD 25)
Step/strategy articles: prowritingaid, writer.com, udemy blog, tckpublishing, grammar-monster "how to improve your spelling", EF English Live, Espresso English "5 rules", U of Toronto "Seven Strategies" (ELL), SkillsYouNeed, British Council India. Recurring steps: learn rules + exceptions; look-cover-write-check; personal trouble-word list; read widely; pick British or American and stick to it; proofread without trusting spell-check. Recurring words: separate, definitely, accommodate, receive, necessary.
Question signals: "How can I spell correctly?", "Should I use British or American spelling?", "Can I rely on spell check?"

Quality notes: Leverage Edu lists talk/silk as silent-k, "Halk/Palk" non-words, "wreck" as silent k. Testbook misspells "gregerious" in an answer. BYJU'S FAQ says "5 rules" while the body has 12. FluentU promises 50 words, delivers ~47. Four of eight pages show no author or date.

### Patterns
- Rules guides converge on the same 8–12 rules (y+suffix, -es plurals, drop silent e, i-before-e, f→ves, doubling, -ful/-ly, al-, q+u, -ck after short vowel, prefixes don't change the base).
- Rules pages have no misspelled-word list; misspelled-word pages have no rules. Nobody fuses rule → the real words it saves you on → self-test.
- Hard-word listicles pick spelling-bee words, not words adults type in emails.
- Interactivity is mostly off-page or gated (PDF behind email/OTP). Grammar Monster is the only one with an on-page test.
- Only Grammar Monster ranks words by actual misspelling frequency.
- Zero pages address Hindi/L1 interference or British-vs-American choice for Indian workplaces.

### Gaps for Hindi-medium adults and email writers
1. Phonetic-script transfer: Hindi is written as it sounds, so learners write English the same way → untill, tommorow, goverment, seperate, definately. Nobody explains the cause or the fix (anchor the silent/unstressed letter to a related word: govern-ment, de-finite-ly).
2. Indian-English pronunciation-driven errors: v/w, s/sh (shedule), -er/-or/-ar endings (calender, grammer), schedule, Wednesday, colleague, queue.
3. Email-vocabulary audit: the 40–60 words that recur in Indian workplace emails and get misspelled (receive, acknowledge, occurred, accommodate, necessary, immediately, sincerely, regards, apologies, convenience, available, maintenance, recommend, until, business, calendar, address, schedule, liaise, guarantee, questionnaire, grateful, truly, argument, judgement).
4. British vs American: Indian firms default to British (colour, organisation, programme, licence/license, practise/practice) while Gmail/Outlook often default to US. Nobody shows how to set spell-check language or which to pick.
5. Spell-check blind spots: homophones/real-word errors (their/there, then/than, lose/loose, affect/effect, principal/principle, complement/compliment).
6. SMS/Hinglish shortenings (pls, u, ur, thru, tmrw) leaking into formal mail.
7. Adult practice routine (5 words/day, say-cover-write-check on your own drafts, dictate-then-type with an AI coach) — the practice SERP is child-centric.
8. Pronunciation-to-spelling bridge: SpeakX can uniquely frame "say it right, spell it right".

### 5. Why impressions but no clicks for "practice spelling" / "practicing spelling"
- Intent mismatch: the SERP is tools, quizzes, printables and games; SpeakX's snippet ("Everything You Need to Know", "Key Benefits", "Career Growth and Salary Impact") promises reading, not doing. The page has 0 word lists, 0 exercises, 0 downloads.
- Audience mismatch: most searchers are parents/teachers of children; the snippet talks about Indian professionals and salary. The adult minority wants exercises, which the page lacks.
- At ~8 among recognisable tool brands (SpellQuiz, Spelling Stars, Twinkl, Education.com), the snippet must out-promise them and currently doesn't.
- The impression drop (2,663 → 1,419 at steady ~8) looks like Google narrowing the query set the page is relevant to after a SERP shift; a page with no practice content is fragile on "practice" queries. Position held, so this is coverage, not a penalty.
- Current FAQ headings are keyword-stuffed ("What are the best ways to practice English spelling practice?"), which degrades snippets.

On-page changes to earn the click:
- Add H2 "Practice spelling: 3 self-tests" with a 20-word dictation list (reveal answers), a 10-question spot-the-misspelling MCQ, and a fix-this-email paragraph. Put "practice" and the number in the heading for jump-links.
- Ungated downloadable one-page practice sheet, linked above the fold; say "free practice sheet" in the meta.
- Rewrite title/meta to promise rules + word list + tests.
- Move the 50-word misspelled-words table to position 2–3 on the page so a table snippet can win.
- FAQPage + HowTo schema.
- Replace the six-method marketing comparison table with practice content; keep the SpeakX CTA after the practice section.
- Say "for adults" in H1/intro to claim the underserved adult slice of a child-centric SERP.

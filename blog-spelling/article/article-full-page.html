English Spelling Rules and Practice: Write Correct Spelling Every Time

By Supriya Manchanda · SpeakX · Updated 17 November 2026 · ~14 min read

English spelling rules are the fastest way to fix spelling for good, because a rule covers hundreds of words while a word list covers only itself. This guide gives Hindi-medium adults and working professionals the ten rules that matter, the 40 words most often misspelled in Indian work emails, the mistakes that come specifically from thinking in Hindi, a six-step method to write correct spelling, and a 7-day practice plan with self-tests you can do in ten minutes a day.

To write correct spelling in English, learn the ten rules that cover most words, keep a personal list of the words you get wrong, say each word in syllables before you write it, and practice spelling for ten minutes a day with a self-test. Hindi speakers also need to unlearn the habit of spelling by sound, because English is not phonetic.

Why is English spelling hard for Hindi speakers?

Hindi is written the way it sounds; English is not. If you learned to write in Devanagari, you learned that a word is spelled by listening to it, and that habit works against you in English. “Receive”, “believe” and “key” share one sound and three spellings. “Knowledge”, “debt” and “receipt” contain letters nobody says. “Grammar” and “calendar” sound as if they end in -er. Spelling by ear produces recieve, seperate and tommorow, which are the three most common errors in Indian office email.

The good news is that English spelling is irregular, not random. About ten rules explain most words, and the exceptions that matter at work fit on one page. Learn the rules, keep a list of your own exceptions, and spelling stops being a daily guess.

The 10 English spelling rules that cover most words

These are the rules worth memorizing. Each one comes with the examples you are most likely to type at work and the exceptions you should know.

Rule 1: i before e, except after c

Examples: believe, achieve, friend, receive, ceiling, deceive

The rule works when the sound is “ee”. After c, write ei: receive, receipt. Exceptions you will meet at work: weird, seize, their, foreign, height.

Rule 2: Drop the silent e before a vowel suffix

Examples: make → making, write → writing, hope → hoping, use → usable

Keep the e before a consonant suffix: hopeful, useless, safely. Keep it after a soft c or g before -able: noticeable, manageable.

Rule 3: Double the final consonant in short stressed syllables

Examples: stop → stopped, plan → planning, begin → beginning, occur → occurred

One vowel, one final consonant, stress on the last syllable: double it. Do not double when the stress is earlier: open → opening, visit → visited.

Rule 4: Change y to i after a consonant

Examples: apply → applied, happy → happiness, carry → carries

Keep the y before -ing (applying, carrying) and when a vowel comes before it: play → played, enjoy → enjoyed.

Rule 5: -tion, -sion and -cian follow the base word

Examples: inform → information, decide → decision, electric → electrician

Most nouns take -tion. Use -sion after d, s or when the base ends in -de or -mit: extend → extension, permit → permission. Use -cian for people.

Rule 6: Plurals that are not just -s

Examples: box → boxes, baby → babies, knife → knives, criterion → criteria

Add -es after s, x, ch, sh (buses, batches). Nouns ending in consonant + y take -ies. Watch Latin and Greek plurals used at work: data, criteria, media.

Rule 7: Silent letters are fixed, not optional

Examples: knowledge, debt, receipt, island, Wednesday, government

You cannot hear the k, b, p, s, d or n, so sound alone will not help. Learn these words by sight and by their roots: receipt comes from receive, debt from debit.

Rule 8: Prefixes do not change the base word

Examples: mis + spell = misspell, un + necessary = unnecessary, dis + appear = disappear

Keep both letters where the prefix ends with the letter the base starts with. This is why misspell has two s’s and unnecessary has two n’s.

Rule 9: -ful, -ly and -ness have fixed forms

Examples: careful (one l), carefully (two l’s), beautiful, happiness

The suffix -ful always has one l. Adding -ly to a word ending in l gives two l’s: real → really, final → finally.

Rule 10: British and American spellings are both correct, but pick one

Examples: colour / color, organise / organize, centre / center, programme / program

Indian offices mostly use British spelling. Whichever you choose, keep it consistent within one email or document, and match your company’s style guide if it has one.

Spelling tips for Hindi speakers

Spelling is वर्तनी (vartani) in Hindi, and in Hindi it is almost automatic because the script records every sound. These English mistakes are not about carelessness; they come from Hindi phonology meeting English spelling. Knowing the cause makes the fix stick.

  • Weak final vowels: -ar, -er, -or. In speech these endings sound the same (grammar, teacher, doctor), and Hindi gives no clue. Learn them in groups: -ar words (grammar, calendar, similar, regular), -or words (doctor, author, visitor, monitor). Everything else is usually -er.
  • Double letters you cannot hear. Hindi marks a doubled consonant with a visible sign (क्क); English does not change the sound much. Address, necessary, accommodate, committee, occurred: learn these as pictures, and use Rule 3 for the ones that come from adding a suffix.
  • v and w. Hindi has one sound (व) for both letters, so very, wary, vest and west get mixed. Remember that English w words are usually old everyday words (water, work, week, window) and v words are often from Latin or French (value, visit, very, vacancy).
  • s, sh and c. Words like decision, precision and conscious have a “sh” sound spelled with s, c or sc. Hindi speakers often write them with sh. Rule 5 helps: decide → decision, not decishion.
  • Silent letters. Hindi writes every sound, so a silent k, b or p feels wrong. Write silent-letter words from their roots: debt from debit, receipt from receive, sign from signature, where the letter is pronounced.
  • Loanwords that look Hindi-shaped. Words that Hindi has borrowed are often respelled in Hinglish: “collage” for college, “schedual” for schedule, “restaurent” for restaurant. The English spelling is fixed; check it once and add it to your list.
  • SMS spellings leaking into formal mail. pls, u, ur, thru, tmrw and 2moro are fine in a chat with friends and wrong in a work email. Type the full word every time in email, even to colleagues; the habit is what matters.
  • Translating the sound of a Hindi word. When you think “पता” and write “address”, you are translating meaning, not sound, so keep it that way. The error appears when you write an English word the way you would transliterate it into Devanagari (for example “enviroment” because the second n is barely said). Say the word in slow syllables before writing it.

How to write correct spelling in English: a six-step method

Use this every time you are unsure of a word. It takes a few seconds and it trains the habit that eventually makes checking unnecessary.

  1. Say it in syllables. en-vi-ron-ment, gov-ern-ment, def-i-nite-ly. Most dropped letters live in the syllable you swallow when speaking fast.
  2. Ask which rule applies. Suffix added? Check Rules 2, 3, 4 and 9. “ee” sound? Rule 1. Noun from a verb? Rule 5.
  3. Picture the word. Close your eyes and see it written. Words you read often are stored as shapes; the shape will usually tell you if an ending looks wrong.
  4. Write it, then read it backwards. Reading a word letter by letter from the end stops your brain from auto-correcting what it expects to see.
  5. Check it once, properly. Use a dictionary or your own list, not a guess. Set the spell-check language in Gmail, Outlook, Word and your phone keyboard to English (India) or English (UK) so it stops flagging British spellings and starts catching real errors. Autocorrect still misses a correctly spelled wrong word (form / from, than / then, their / there); the list below covers those.
  6. Use it the same day. Put the word in a real sentence, ideally in an email you are sending anyway. A word used is a word remembered.

Mistakes spell-check will not catch

These are real words, so no red line appears. They reach your manager’s inbox more often than any misspelling. Check them by meaning, not by sound.

  • their / there / they’re: their report (belongs to them), over there (place), they’re late (they are).
  • then / than: then is time (first this, then that); than compares (better than).
  • lose / loose: lose a client (one o); a loose screw (two o’s).
  • affect / effect: affect is the verb (it affects sales); effect is the noun (the effect on sales).
  • principal / principle: principal is the main one or the head of a college; a principle is a rule.
  • complement / compliment: complement completes (the chart complements the data); a compliment is praise.
  • form / from: the most common typo in Indian email; read the sentence aloud and it jumps out.
  • its / it’s: it’s only ever means it is or it has; its shows belonging.
  • advice / advise: advice is the noun (take my advice); advise is the verb (please advise).
  • stationary / stationery: stationary means not moving; stationery is pens and paper (e for envelope).

Practice spelling: five exercises you can do in ten minutes

Practicing spelling only works when you test recall, not recognition. Reading a list feels productive but changes nothing; writing from memory and checking is what moves a word into long-term memory. Use these five exercises, ten minutes a day.

  1. Cover-and-write. Cover the “correct spelling” column of the table above, write the 40 words, check, and mark the misses. Repeat the misses the next day.
  2. Dictation by a partner or an app. Have a colleague, a family member or a text-to-speech app read ten words aloud while you write them. This is the exercise most adults never do after school, and it is the most effective one.
  3. Proofread one real email. Before you hit send, read it once only for spelling, backwards from the last word. Add anything you doubted to your list, even if it turned out right.
  4. Rule-of-the-day. Pick one rule, find five words in your own writing that use it, and write each one three times with the rule in mind.
  5. The personal list. Keep a note on your phone of every word you have ever got wrong at work. Test yourself on the whole list every Sunday; words you spell right three times in a row graduate off it.

Frequently asked questions

What are the 5 basic spelling rules in English?

The five rules that fix the most mistakes are: i before e except after c (believe, receive); drop the silent e before a vowel suffix (make, making); double the final consonant in a stressed short syllable (begin, beginning); change y to i after a consonant (apply, applied); and keep the base word when you add a prefix (mis + spell = misspell). The full list of ten rules above adds plurals, silent letters, -tion/-sion, fixed suffixes and British versus American spelling.

How can I practice spelling as an adult?

Keep a personal list of words you actually get wrong, test yourself on it twice a week by covering the correct spelling and writing the words, have someone dictate ten words a day, and proofread one real email daily. Ten minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday, and words from your own emails stick better than random lists.

How do I write correct spelling in English?

Say the word in syllables, think of the rule it follows, picture the word, write it, then check it against a dictionary or your list. Over a few weeks the checking step disappears for the words you use most. The six-step method above walks through this.

Which are the most difficult spellings in English?

Words with silent letters (knowledge, receipt, government), double letters (accommodate, necessary, embarrass), and endings that sound alike (-ance/-ence, -ar/-er) cause the most mistakes. The table of 40 commonly misspelled words above covers the ones that appear most in work emails.

Why is English spelling hard for Hindi speakers?

Spelling (वर्तनी, vartani) in Hindi follows the sound, so Hindi speakers naturally spell English by sound too. English is not phonetic: the same sound has several spellings (receive, believe, key) and many letters are silent. The fix is to learn the rules and the common exceptions by sight, not to listen harder.

Does spelling matter in work emails?

Yes. A misspelled word in a client email or a job application is the first thing many readers notice, and it suggests the rest was not checked. Correct spelling will not win the deal on its own, but a misspelled one can lose attention before your point lands. Spell-check does not catch real-word errors such as form for from or than for then, so a final read is still needed.

Should I use British or American spelling in India?

Either is correct. Most Indian companies, government forms and newspapers use British spelling (colour, organise, centre). Pick one, keep it consistent in a document, and follow your company’s style if it has one. Set your email and phone keyboard to the same variety so autocorrect helps instead of fighting you.

Can an app help me practice spelling?

Yes, for dictation and self-tests. Any app that reads a word aloud and checks what you type works as a practice partner.

## Why should you learn daily use English sentences instead of single words?

Because conversations happen in sentences, not words. Knowing help, you and please is not the same as saying “Can you help me, please?” quickly when a stranger is waiting. Ready-made sentences give you patterns you can reuse, so you respond without building each line from scratch.

## Daily use English sentences in Marathi: how to say them, not just read them

Reading the Marathi meaning is not the same as speaking the English sentence. Most lists of daily use English sentences in Marathi give you understanding; your mouth still needs practice. Use this three-step script for every group of ten:

- Cover the Marathi column. Look only at the English sentence and the “when to use” note.
- Say the English line twice, aloud. The first time slowly, the second time at normal speed. If you are not sure of the meaning, uncover the Marathi, check, and say it again.
- Use it in a two-line roleplay. Shop: “How much is this?” “It is two hundred rupees.” Phone: “I am on my way.” “Okay, I will wait.” Office: “I am working on it.” “Thanks, let me know when it is done.”

Ten sentences this way take about ten minutes. Do it with the group you need most this week, then add the 15-minute routine below. When you want feedback on how the sentences sound, say them to SpeakX, which listens and tells you how your fluency and pronunciation landed.

## What mistakes do Marathi speakers make with daily English sentences?

Most of these come from carrying a Marathi pattern into English. They are not grammar-book errors, but they can sound off in an interview or on a client call.

**Common patterns and the natural sentence**
| Commonly said | Say this instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I am having a doubt. | I have a question. | “Doubt” means you do not believe something; state verbs do not take -ing. |
| What is your good name? | What is your name? | A direct translation of “तुमचं शुभ नाव”; it confuses listeners outside India. |
| He is my cousin brother. | He is my cousin. | English “cousin” already covers मावस भाऊ / चुलत भाऊ; “brother” is extra. |
| I will come at sharp 10. | I will come at 10 sharp. | “Sharp” goes after the time. |
| She told me that come tomorrow. | She told me to come tomorrow. | Reported requests use “told me to”, not “that” plus a command, which mirrors “ये असं सांगितलं”. |
| Today morning I called. | This morning I called. | “आज सकाळी” becomes “this morning”, not “today morning”. |
| Please revert back. | Please reply. | “Revert” means to go back to an earlier state; “reply” is the word you need. |

Pronunciation has its own set. Marathi speakers often swap v and w (very, wine) and pronounce z as j (zero, zoo); these words are worth practicing on their own. ## How can I learn five English sentences every day?

Pick five sentences from one situation, read each aloud three times, change one word in each to make your own version, and use at least one in a real conversation before the day ends. Five sentences a day means the whole list in twenty days, and sentences you have actually said are the ones you remember.

### Step 1: Five from one situation
Do not jump across groups. Five office sentences on a work day, five shopping sentences before you go to the market.

### Step 2: Say each aloud
Three times, out loud, not silently. Your mouth needs the practice as much as your memory does.

### Step 3: Make one variation
From “I am working on it” you get “I am working on the report” and “I am working on the presentation”. The pattern is worth more than the sentence.

### Step 4: Use one in real life
With a colleague, a shopkeeper, a bus conductor or at home. The 10-day tracker in the PDF covers ten sentences a day; after day 10, start again with the groups you found hardest for days 11 to 20.

## How can I stop translating every sentence from Marathi in my head?

Translating helps the first time you meet a sentence, but speaking becomes slow if you translate every line while talking. The fix is patterns. Once you understand the Marathi meaning of “I need some water” (मला थोडं पाणी हवं आहे), stop translating and learn I need… as a ready-made opening: I need some help. I need some time. I need one more day. Do the same with Can you…?: Can you help me? Can you show me? Can you repeat that, please? After a week of saying the pattern aloud, the English comes first.

## How can I practice daily use English sentences at home?

### How do I make a Marathi sentence in English?
Start from the pattern, not the words. Find the English sentence that does the same job as your Marathi one (a request, a question, a status) and reuse its shape: “मला पाणी हवं आहे” is “I need some water”, so “मला वेळ हवा आहे” becomes “I need some time”. Word-by-word translation gives sentences like “I am having a doubt”; pattern practice gives natural English.

### Are the Marathi meanings literal translations?
No. Each Marathi meaning is the natural way a Marathi speaker would say the same thing, in the polite तुम्ही form, not a word-for-word translation. Where the verb changes with the speaker, both forms are given, for example थकलो आहे / थकले आहे. The transliteration column shows the Marathi as it is commonly typed.
### Should I use तुम्ही or तू?
The Marathi column uses the polite तुम्ही form, which is safe with anyone. With close friends or younger family you can switch to तू in Marathi, but the English sentence stays the same, because English “you” does not change.

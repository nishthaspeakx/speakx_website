Why should you learn daily use English sentences instead of single words?

Because conversations happen in sentences, not words. Knowing help, you and please is not the same as saying “Can you help me, please?” quickly when a stranger is waiting. Ready-made sentences give you patterns you can reuse, so you respond without building each line from scratch.

For Tamil-medium learners, the Tamil column is a first step, not a crutch. Read the Tamil meaning once to be sure of the sense, then say the English sentence until it comes on its own. What are the 100 most useful English sentences for daily use?

The 100 most useful daily use English sentences are short, polite and practical: greetings such as “How are you?”, home sentences such as “The food is ready”, office sentences such as “I am working on it”, help requests such as “Please speak slowly”, and goodbyes such as “Take care”. Below they are grouped into ten situations, with the Tamil meaning, a transliteration and a note on when to use each one. Which English sentences are most useful for beginners?

If you are a complete beginner, start with these ten. They cover greeting someone, asking for help, slowing a conversation down and ending it politely, which is most of what a first conversation needs.

  1. How are you? — நீங்கள் எப்படி இருக்கிறீர்கள்?
  2. I am fine, thank you. — நான் நலமாக இருக்கிறேன், நன்றி.
  3. What are you doing? — நீங்கள் என்ன செய்து கொண்டிருக்கிறீர்கள்?
  4. Where are you going? — நீங்கள் எங்கே போகிறீர்கள்?
  5. Can you help me? — நீங்கள் எனக்கு உதவ முடியுமா?
  6. I don’t understand. — எனக்குப் புரியவில்லை.
  7. Please speak slowly. — தயவுசெய்து மெதுவாகப் பேசுங்கள்.
  8. Can you repeat that, please? — தயவுசெய்து மீண்டும் சொல்ல முடியுமா?
  9. I will call you later. — நான் பின்னர் உங்களை அழைக்கிறேன்.
  10. Thank you for your help. — உங்கள் உதவிக்கு நன்றி.

Spoken English sentences in Tamil: how to say these aloud

Reading the Tamil meaning is not the same as speaking the English sentence. Spoken English sentences in Tamil, the way most videos present them, give you understanding; your mouth still needs practice. Use this three-step script for every group of ten:

  1. Cover the Tamil column. Look only at the English sentence and the “when to use” note.
  2. Say the English line twice, aloud. The first time slowly, the second time at normal speed. If you are not sure of the meaning, uncover the Tamil, check, and say it again.
  3. Use it in a two-line roleplay. Shop: “How much is this?” “It is two hundred rupees.” Phone: “I am on my way.” “Okay, I will wait.” Office: “I am working on it.” “Thanks, let me know when it is done.”

What mistakes do Tamil speakers make with daily English sentences?

Most of these come from carrying a Tamil pattern into English. They are not grammar-book errors, but they can sound off in an interview or on a client call.

How can I learn five English sentences every day?

Pick five sentences from one situation, read each aloud three times, change one word in each to make your own version, and use at least one in a real conversation before the day ends. Five sentences a day means the whole list in twenty days, and sentences you have actually said are the ones you remember.

Step 1: Five from one situation

Do not jump across groups. Five office sentences on a work day, five shopping sentences before you go to the market.

Step 2: Say each aloud

Three times, out loud, not silently. Your mouth needs the practice as much as your memory does.

Step 3: Make one variation

From “I am working on it” you get “I am working on the report” and “I am working on the presentation”. The pattern is worth more than the sentence.

Step 4: Use one in real life

With a colleague, a shopkeeper, a bus conductor or at home. The 10-day tracker in the PDF covers ten sentences a day; after day 10, start again with the groups you found hardest for days 11 to 20.

How can I stop translating every sentence from Tamil in my head?

Translating helps the first time you meet a sentence, but speaking becomes slow if you translate every line while talking. The fix is patterns. Once you understand the Tamil meaning of “I need some water” (எனக்குக் கொஞ்சம் தண்ணீர் வேண்டும்), stop translating and learn I need… as a ready-made opening: I need some help. I need some time. I need one more day. Do the same with Can you…?: Can you help me? Can you show me? Can you repeat that, please? After a week of saying the pattern aloud, the English comes first.

How can I practice daily use English sentences at home?

Practice at home by reading the sentences aloud, recording yourself on your phone, making your own variations, and running imaginary conversations: order food from an imaginary waiter, give directions to an imaginary auto driver in Chennai or Coimbatore. A partner or a speaking app adds the one thing you cannot give yourself: feedback. Our guide to practicing spoken English alone at home has the full routine.

  • 5 minutes, converse: use them in a short conversation with a partner or a speaking-practice app.

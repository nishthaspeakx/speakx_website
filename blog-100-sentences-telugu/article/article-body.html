Why should you learn daily use English sentences instead of single words?

Because conversations happen in sentences, not words. Knowing help, you and please is not the same as saying “Can you help me, please?” quickly when a stranger is waiting. Ready-made sentences give you patterns you can reuse, so you respond without building each line from scratch.

For Telugu-medium learners, the Telugu column is a first step, not a crutch. Read the Telugu meaning once to be sure of the sense, then say the English sentence until it comes on its own. Which English sentences are most useful for beginners?

If you are a complete beginner, start with these ten. They cover greeting someone, asking for help, slowing a conversation down and ending it politely, which is most of what a first conversation needs.

  1. How are you? — మీరు ఎలా ఉన్నారు?
  2. I am fine, thank you. — నేను బాగున్నాను, ధన్యవాదాలు.
  3. What are you doing? — మీరు ఏం చేస్తున్నారు?
  4. Where are you going? — మీరు ఎక్కడికి వెళ్తున్నారు?
  5. Can you help me? — మీరు నాకు సహాయం చేయగలరా?
  6. I don’t understand. — నాకు అర్థం కాలేదు.
  7. Please speak slowly. — దయచేసి నెమ్మదిగా మాట్లాడండి.
  8. Can you repeat that, please? — దయచేసి మళ్ళీ చెప్పగలరా?
  9. I will call you later. — నేను తర్వాత మీకు ఫోన్ చేస్తాను.
  10. Thank you for your help. — మీ సహాయానికి ధన్యవాదాలు.

Telugu to English sentences: how to say them, not just read them

Reading the Telugu meaning is not the same as speaking the English sentence. Most Telugu to English sentences online, in videos and on Pinterest, give you understanding; your mouth still needs practice. Use this three-step script for every group of ten:

  1. Cover the Telugu column. Look only at the English sentence and the “when to use” note.
  2. Say the English line twice, aloud. The first time slowly, the second time at normal speed. If you are not sure of the meaning, uncover the Telugu, check, and say it again.
  3. Use it in a two-line roleplay. Shop: “How much is this?” “It is two hundred rupees.” Phone: “I am on my way.” “Okay, I will wait.” Office: “I am working on it.” “Thanks, let me know when it is done.”

Ten sentences this way take about ten minutes. Do it with the group you need most this week, then add the 15-minute routine below. When you want feedback on how the sentences sound, say them to SpeakX, which listens and tells you how your fluency and pronunciation landed.

What mistakes do Telugu speakers make with daily English sentences?

Most of these come from carrying a Telugu pattern into English. They are not grammar-book errors, but they can sound off in an interview or on a client call.

How can I learn five English sentences every day?

Pick five sentences from one situation, read each aloud three times, change one word in each to make your own version, and use at least one in a real conversation before the day ends. Five sentences a day means the whole list in twenty days, and sentences you have actually said are the ones you remember.

Step 1: Five from one situation

Do not jump across groups. Five office sentences on a work day, five shopping sentences before you go to the market.

Step 2: Say each aloud

Three times, out loud, not silently. Your mouth needs the practice as much as your memory does.

Step 3: Make one variation

From “I am working on it” you get “I am working on the report” and “I am working on the presentation”. The pattern is worth more than the sentence.

Step 4: Use one in real life

With a colleague, a shopkeeper, a bus conductor or at home. The 10-day tracker in the PDF covers ten sentences a day; after day 10, start again with the groups you found hardest for days 11 to 20.

How can I stop translating every sentence from Telugu in my head?

Translating helps the first time you meet a sentence, but speaking becomes slow if you translate every line while talking. The fix is patterns. Once you understand the Telugu meaning of “I need some water” (నాకు కొంచెం నీళ్ళు కావాలి), stop translating and learn I need… as a ready-made opening: I need some help. I need some time. I need one more day. Do the same with Can you…?: Can you help me? Can you show me? Can you repeat that, please? After a week of saying the pattern aloud, the English comes first.

Are the Telugu meanings literal translations?

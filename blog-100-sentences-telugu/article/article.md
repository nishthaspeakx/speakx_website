## What mistakes do Telugu speakers make with daily English sentences?

Most of these come from carrying a Telugu pattern into English. They are not grammar-book errors, but they can sound off in an interview or on a client call.

**Common patterns and the natural sentence**
| Commonly said | Say this instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I am having a doubt. | I have a question. | “Doubt” means you do not believe something; state verbs do not take -ing. |
| He is my cousin brother. | He is my cousin. | English “cousin” already covers మేనమామ కొడుకు / అత్త కొడుకు; “brother” is extra. |
| What is your native place? | Where are you from? | Natural English asks where you are from; “native place” is a direct translation of స్వస్థలం. |
| I will come at sharp 10. | I will come at 10 sharp. | “Sharp” goes after the time. |
| She told me that come tomorrow. | She told me to come tomorrow. | Reported requests use “told me to”, not “that” plus a command, which mirrors రమ్మని చెప్పింది. |
| I am going to office daily morning. | I go to the office every morning. | Routines use the simple present; “daily morning” is a direct translation of రోజూ ఉదయం. |
| Kindly revert back. | Please reply. | “Revert” means to go back to an earlier state; “reply” is the word you need. |

## How can I learn five English sentences every day?

Pick five sentences from one situation, read each aloud three times, change one word in each to make your own version, and use at least one in a real conversation before the day ends. Five sentences a day means the whole list in twenty days, and sentences you have actually said are the ones you remember.

### Step 1: Five from one situation
Do not jump across groups. Five office sentences on a work day, five shopping sentences before you go to the market.

### Step 2: Say each aloud
Three times, out loud, not silently. Your mouth needs the practice as much as your memory does.

### Step 3: Make one variation
From “I am working on it” you get “I am working on the report” and “I am working on the presentation”. The pattern is worth more than the sentence.

### Step 4: Use one in real life
With a colleague, a shopkeeper, a bus conductor or at home. The 10-day tracker in the PDF covers ten sentences a day; after day 10, start again with the groups you found hardest for days 11 to 20.

## How can I stop translating every sentence from Telugu in my head?

Translating helps the first time you meet a sentence, but speaking becomes slow if you translate every line while talking. The fix is patterns. Once you understand the Telugu meaning of “I need some water” (నాకు కొంచెం నీళ్ళు కావాలి), stop translating and learn I need… as a ready-made opening: I need some help. I need some time. I need one more day. Do the same with Can you…?: Can you help me? Can you show me? Can you repeat that, please? After a week of saying the pattern aloud, the English comes first.
